Privacy policy

Last updated 15 November 2026

Doornote reminds you of a checklist when you leave a place you saved. It is built to work without an account and without a server of its own. This page lists every place your data can go.

In short

  • Your doors, lists and exit times are stored only on your phone. We have no server and receive none of it.
  • Your phone’s operating system detects when you leave; Doornote does not keep a location history.
  • Three outside services are involved: the map provider (Google Maps on Android, Apple Maps on iPhone) when you look at the map, RevenueCat when you buy or restore Premium, and Expo when the app checks for updates.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking across apps, nothing sold.

What stays on your phone

Doornote stores the following in its own storage on your device:

  • Each door you save: its name, its location (latitude and longitude) and the reminder distance.
  • Your lists and their items.
  • The time of your last exits and returns, and a short event log used by the Diagnostics screen.
  • Your settings, such as language and whether you own Premium.

None of this is sent to us. Deleting a door deletes its data; uninstalling the app deletes all of it. If you have phone backups turned on (Google backup on Android, iCloud on iPhone), your phone may include this app data in your own backup, which Google or Apple store for you under their policies.

Location

Doornote asks for location access “Always” (Android: “Allow all the time”) because the reminder has to work while the app is closed and your phone is in your pocket.

The app registers each door with your phone’s geofencing service (Google Play services location on Android, Core Location on iPhone). The phone watches the distance and tells Doornote only that you left or came back. Doornote does not record where you go.

When you save a door with “I’m home now” or “Use current location”, the app asks the phone for your current position once. These location services are part of your phone’s operating system and may process location data under Google’s or Apple’s privacy policies, depending on your device settings.

Maps

When you place a door on the map, the map is drawn by Google Maps on Android and by Apple Maps on iPhone. To show it, the map provider receives requests for the map area on screen, together with standard technical data such as your IP address and device type. This lets the provider infer the approximate area you are looking at. Doornote does not send your list or your exit times to the map provider.

See the Google Privacy Policy and Apple Privacy Policy.

Purchases

Premium is a one-time purchase made through the App Store or Google Play. Doornote uses RevenueCat to handle the purchase and to check whether you own Premium.

For this, the app connects to RevenueCat, which receives a random anonymous ID created on your phone, your purchase receipt and product, and technical data such as app version, device type, country or currency, and IP address. Payment itself is handled by Apple or Google; we never see your card details. See the RevenueCat Privacy Policy.

App updates

Doornote can receive small updates without a new store release. When it starts, the app asks Expo’s update service (EAS Update) whether an update is available. The request contains the app version, platform and similar technical data, plus your IP address; it contains none of your doors, lists or locations. See the Expo Privacy Policy.

Notifications

Reminders are created on your phone. Doornote does not use a push notification service and has no way to message you from outside the app.

Diagnostic report

Settings › Diagnostics can build a text report to help find out why a reminder did not arrive. It contains your device type and system version, the app version, permission states, your door names and distances, recent exit times and the event log. It is never sent automatically: it goes only where you share it.

What we don’t do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No advertising, and no advertising ID.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting service.
  • No tracking across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • We do not sell or share your data.

Children

Doornote is a general-audience app and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone.

Your choices

  • Turn location or notifications off at any time in your phone’s settings. Reminders stop until you turn them back on.
  • Delete a door, or uninstall the app, to delete its data from your phone.
  • For data held by Google, Apple or RevenueCat, use their privacy tools.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date. If the app ever starts sending data somewhere new, the policy will say so before that version is released.
